01 / ProjectEvery piece,
a second life.

A compact kitchen module that brings salvaged materials into everyday use. An Oregon timber benchtop, ceramic tile inlays, plywood storage and an aluminium frame form a small, expressive object shaped by the pieces available.

A removable charcoal-grill tray draws on Vietnamese cooking practices. The tile inlays create dedicated pot-rest surfaces, while the drawer and lower storage keep the module practical and compact.

02 / Material approachDesigning
with what exists.

The design began with collected materials rather than a fixed specification. Workshop offcuts, salvaged timber and tiles informed the module’s proportions, surfaces and connections. Visible differences in grain, colour and texture remain part of its character.

04 / Built prototypeAn object
for everyday use.

The full-scale prototype tests how the components meet and how the object is used: opening the drawer, accessing storage and lifting out the metal tray.

Photography: Dien Pham & Quan Pham.

05 / Wider investigationFrom object
to architecture.

The accompanying Reframing Studio proposal extends the material-reuse investigation to the scale of a timber facility. Mapping, material flows and architectural drawings explore how reclaimed timber and urban trees can enter new cycles of use.

The kitchen module is presented here alongside this wider studio work as a tangible exploration of assembling available materials.
